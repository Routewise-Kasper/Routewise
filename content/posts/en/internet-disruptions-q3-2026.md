Cloudflare's quarterly report counts **49 notable internet disruptions** observed on its Radar platform in Q3 2026.

## By cause

| Cause | Count | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Government-directed shutdowns | 19 | 18 were exam-related shutdowns in Iraq |
| Power outages | 14 | Across 10 countries |
| Natural disasters | 5 | Earthquakes, typhoons, floods |
| Other | — | Conflict, cable cuts, cyberattacks |

Shutdowns were the most frequent cause, driven by Iraq's scheduled exam shutdowns, which lasted roughly 15 to 90 minutes each and mostly fell between August 26 and September 28.

## Notable events

**Cuba:** four power-related outages, including a three-day blackout from September 18 to 20, with traffic drops between 18% and 59%.

**Ethiopia (Tigray):** a 10-day government-directed shutdown. Regional traffic fell 100%, and national HTTP traffic dropped up to 30%.

**Honduras:** a failure at a power interconnection facility on September 10 cut national HTTP traffic by 63% for about three hours.

**Natural disasters:** a magnitude 7.4 earthquake in Colombia caused a roughly 4.5-day disruption, with fiber damage contributing. Japan's magnitude 7.1 quake in late July mainly hit desktop traffic, while mobile held up.

**Data centers:** on August 13, a cooling failure at a Phoenix data center caused a near-total traffic drop for Namecheap.

## Most disruptions were short

Of the 49, 14 returned to normal traffic within an hour and 16 within three hours.

## What it means for you

The internet runs on power, cables and buildings, and any of them can fail. A little preparation helps:

- **Keep offline copies** of maps and important documents
- **Have more than one way to connect**, such as mobile data plus home broadband
- **Research your destination** before you travel. See [Traveling to China? Set Up Your Internet Before You Fly](/en/guides/china-travel-internet/)
