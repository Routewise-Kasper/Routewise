You're on a layover, your phone is at 30%, and there's a network called "Airport_Free_WiFi." Most people just tap it. Most of the time nothing bad happens, but "most of the time" deserves a closer look.

The reassuring part first: nearly every site and app now uses HTTPS, so someone else on the same Wi‑Fi has a hard time reading your messages or purchases. The risk has moved elsewhere.

## Where the risk actually is

**The wrong network.** Anyone can create a hotspot called "Starbucks" or "Hotel_Guest" with a laptop. Connect to it and all your traffic goes through their device. It's called an evil twin, and it's cheap to pull off.

**Fake login pages.** Public networks often show a page asking for a room number or phone number. A fake one might ask you to "sign in with Google" and keep whatever you type.

**Your device oversharing.** Phones and laptops look for networks they've joined before, broadcast their names, and sometimes leave file sharing open to everyone nearby.

## Before you connect

Ask staff for the exact network name, including hyphens and capitalization. It's the single most effective step and the one people skip.

Turn off auto-join. On iPhone, tap the "i" next to the network and switch off Auto-Join; on Android, turn off auto-connect in the network details.

Minimize sharing. Set AirDrop to Contacts Only or Receiving Off, and when Windows or macOS asks whether to be discoverable on a public network, say no.

## While you're connected

Give the login page only what it needs. Legitimate pages ask you to accept terms, enter a room number or receive a code. Anything asking for social media passwords or card details is a reason to disconnect.

Finish the login page first, then turn on your VPN. Before you authenticate, most public networks block everything except the login page.

Never click through certificate warnings on familiar sites. "Your connection is not private" on a site you use daily is a red flag.

Do sensitive things on cellular or your own hotspot when you can: bank transfers, password changes, work admin panels.

## After you leave

Forget the network so your device doesn't auto-join a lookalike elsewhere. If you logged into important accounts, check their "devices" or "security" page later and sign out anything unfamiliar.

## Where a VPN helps

A VPN turns everything passing through that hotspot into an encrypted tunnel. Even if you joined a fake network, the operator only sees a connection to a VPN server, not the sites you visit.
