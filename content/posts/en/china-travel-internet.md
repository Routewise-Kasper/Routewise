If you're heading to mainland China for the first time, one thing catches most travelers off guard: many of the apps and sites you use every day, including Google services, WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube and X, are not reachable on local networks.

The good news is that it's easy to prepare. The key is to do it **before you land**.

## Why you need to prepare in advance

Once you're on a mainland network, it becomes much harder to fix things:

- App store listings for many tools may be unavailable or unreachable
- Download pages and help articles may not load
- Your usual search engine and email may not work, so troubleshooting is slow

So treat your phone setup like your passport: sort it out at home.

## Your three options

| Option | How it works | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| International roaming | Data routes through your home carrier | Simple, often works with blocked apps | Can be expensive; check your plan |
| Travel eSIM | Data plan from an eSIM provider | Cheap, quick to set up | Behavior varies by provider |
| Local SIM + VPN | Local data, VPN for foreign services | Fast local apps, low cost | Requires setup before arrival |

Many travelers combine options: a local SIM or eSIM for maps, payments and ride-hailing, plus a VPN installed in advance for everything else.

## Before-you-fly checklist

1. **Install your VPN app** on every device you're bringing, and sign in
2. **Test it at home**, ideally on mobile data, and try a few different server locations
3. **Turn on smart (split) mode** if your app has it, so local apps like maps and payment run directly
4. **Save your account details** offline: login email, and the provider's support contact
5. **Set up local payments**: Alipay and WeChat Pay are used almost everywhere, and both support linking international cards
6. **Download offline maps** and translation packs as a backup

:::tip
Install the app on at least two devices if your plan allows it, for example your phone and laptop. If one has trouble, you still have a way online.
:::

## On the ground: small habits that help

- If a connection drops, switch to a different server before anything else
- Hotel Wi-Fi varies a lot, so mobile data is often more reliable for VPN use
- Keep the app updated, but do updates before your trip when possible
